Suggested Order

If you are not sure where to start, use the prompts in this order. This creates a simple advocacy workflow: first catch what changed, then understand it, then build your response, then organize people around it.

1

Monitor

Start with a policy, legislative, budget, or news prompt to identify what is happening.

2

Translate

Use the Policy Translator to turn dense language into clear explanations for educators and families.

3

Respond

Use the Rapid Response Generator or Event Briefing to create talking points, testimony, emails, or public comments.

4

Organize

Adapt the results for your audience, then share them with members, families, or community partners.

How to Use and Customize These Prompts

These prompts are templates. Copy one, paste it into ChatGPT or another AI tool, and then replace the bracketed placeholder text with your own location, district, bill language, board agenda item, or issue.

  • Replace the location: Change things like [STATE NAME] or [DISTRICT NAME] to your own state, city, district, or region.
  • Paste the actual text: For translator or response prompts, paste in the bill summary, agenda item, or article you want analyzed.
  • Add your issue area: If you care most about ECE, multilingual learners, planning time, or special education, add that directly into the prompt.
  • Adjust the audience: Ask for a version for families, union members, media, legislators, or school board testimony depending on what you need.

The strongest results usually come from doing a quick second pass after the first answer. Ask the AI to make the output shorter, clearer, more parent-friendly, more data-driven, or more specific to your local situation.

  • For beginners: Start with the Universal Policy Monitor and the Policy Translator.
  • For organizers: Use the Budget Watchdog, Event Briefing, and Rapid Response Generator together.
  • For local advocacy: Add your school board, superintendent, district, or state legislature by name.
  • Always verify: Double-check dates, bill numbers, vote status, and public claims before sharing anything publicly.

Universal Policy Monitor

Track legislation, budgets, board actions, and news in one place

Use this when you want one broad monitoring prompt that scans multiple education policy signals at once.

Search for new updates related to public education policy in [STATE, DISTRICT, OR REGION]. Scan the following sources when possible: - state legislature and education committee activity - state department of education releases - school district announcements and budget documents - school board agendas and meeting materials - credible local news coverage Focus on developments involving: - early childhood education (ECE, preschool) - special education and IDEA compliance - multilingual or immersion programs - teacher planning time and workload - staffing levels and educator shortages - class sizes, ratios, and caseload limits - therapist and support staff roles - school funding or budget reductions - curriculum, accountability, or assessment policy - union or labor issues affecting educators Notify me only about meaningful new developments. For each update provide: 1. A short summary of the change 2. The source or policy action 3. Why it matters for educators, students, or public schools 4. Potential advocacy implications

Budget & Staffing Watchdog

Catch cuts, restructures, and workload shifts early

Use this when you need to watch district budgets, staffing changes, service models, or program reductions.

Search for updates from [SCHOOL DISTRICT NAME], the local school board, and the state department of education. Monitor documents, board agendas, and reports for changes related to: - district budgets - staffing levels - program restructuring - class size or ratio changes - caseload limits - service delivery models - program reductions or eliminations - early childhood education programs - special education services - multilingual or immersion programs - teacher planning time or workload Notify me only about meaningful developments. Provide a concise summary and highlight potential impacts on educators, students, and families.
